Privacy policy

Jaddati keeps recordings of people who have died, and creates new speech in their voice. That is sensitive by its nature, so this page says exactly what happens to everything you give it.

Last updated 11 September 2026 · applies to Jaddati for iPhone, version 1.0

The short version

There is no account and no Jaddati server. Recordings, clips, names, relationships and photos are stored in the app's own storage on your iPhone, and nothing is uploaded to us — there is no “us” to upload it to.

Three features cannot work that way, because a company outside your phone performs them. Nothing is sent to either company until you agree to it on the screen that opens the app, and you can withdraw that at any time.

What stays on your phone

  • Every recording you add, and every clip the app creates.
  • Names, relationships and photographs of the people you add.
  • Your interface language, and which person and tab you last had open.
  • Your answer to the data question, and the date you gave it.

These are held in the app's container on the device, which no other app can read. They are not backed up to any service of ours. They are included in your iPhone's own backups if you have those switched on, which is between you and Apple.

What is sent, and to whom

Only after you agree, and only for the feature you are using.

RecipientWhat is sentWhy
ElevenLabs
Voice cloning and speech		 The audio recording you choose, and the text you want spoken in that voice. To build the voice and to read your words in it. The voice it builds is stored under our account there until it is deleted.
Groq
Answers and transcription		 A question typed or spoken during a story, and the text of the page it is about. When you speak instead of typing, the audio of what you said. To write a short spoken answer, and to turn speech into text. Groq is never told whose voice will read the answer out, or the name or relationship of anyone in the app.

Nothing else is sent anywhere. There is no analytics service, no advertising, no crash reporter, no social network, and no third-party SDK of any kind in the app.

A code identifying the device

When Jaddati is configured to reach these services through a relay we operate, requests carry a per-device code so that one phone cannot exhaust the shared allowance. It is Apple's identifier for vendor: it is not a name, it is not linked to one, it is not shared with anyone else, and it disappears when you delete the app. When the app calls ElevenLabs and Groq directly, this code is not sent at all.

Protection by the companies we send to

ElevenLabs and Groq are bound by their own published terms, which require them to protect the data they receive. We have no arrangement with either of them to retain anything on Jaddati's behalf, and Jaddati keeps no copy of what it sends. Their handling of that data is governed by their policies, linked above. Apple requires us to confirm that any third party we share data with provides the same or equal protection as this policy describes, and we rely on those published terms for that confirmation.

Keeping and deleting

  • On the phone. Removing a person deletes their recordings, clips, notes and imported books immediately. Deleting the app removes everything.
  • At the voice service. Removing a person also deletes the cloned voice from ElevenLabs. That happens first; if it fails, nothing is removed on the phone either, so you can try again rather than losing the ability to ask.
  • Requests in flight. Text and audio sent for a single answer are processed and returned. We do not store them.
  • Your answer to the data question is kept until you change it or delete the app.

If you want a voice removed and cannot do it in the app — the phone was lost, or the app was deleted before the voice was — write to the address below with enough detail to identify it and we will delete it.

Withdrawing your agreement

Open Jaddati, tap Privacy and data on the home screen, and choose Stop sending anything off this phone. Every feature that would reach either company stops at once. Playback, the archive, and adding people keep working. You can turn it back on from the same screen.

Recording you are aware of

The microphone is used in two places: recording a voice sample, and speaking instead of typing. In both, a moving level meter and a running clock are on screen the whole time it is open. Jaddati never records in the background and has no way to.

Children

Jaddati is made for families and a child may well be listening to a story in it, but it is not directed at children and is not in the Kids Category. It collects no personal information from anyone, of any age, beyond what is described above. There are no accounts, no profiles, no messaging, and no way for anyone to contact anyone through the app.

Voices of people who have died

Before a voice is created, Jaddati requires you to confirm two things: that you have the right to use the recording and to create new speech in that voice, and that you understand the result is new audio generated by a machine, not something the person actually said. Jaddati cannot verify either, and does not attempt to. If you are not the person entitled to give that permission, please do not use the app for that voice. If you believe a voice has been created from a recording without the right to do so, write to the address below and we will delete it.

If something goes wrong

If data collected through Jaddati is ever exposed, we will say so on this page and in the app the next time it opens. The app holds no email addresses, so there is no list to write to.

Changes

If this policy changes in a way that affects what is sent or to whom, the app asks the question again rather than relying on an answer given to different terms. The date at the top of this page always reflects the current version.
